Five stages, one explainable score

Every analysis runs the same deterministic pipeline, so a judge — or a SOC analyst — can trace exactly how the score of 0–100 was reached.

  1. 01

    Parse

    Reads headers and body with Python's email library, falling back to stripped text for HTML-only messages, and pulls attachments, URLs and IPs.

  2. 02

    Identity check

    Compares From vs. Reply-To domains and display-name branding to catch spoofed or impersonated senders.

  3. 03

    URL & IP intel

    Flags shorteners, punycode homographs, suspicious TLDs, lookalike brand domains, and resolves reverse DNS on public IPs.

  4. 04

    Auth verification

    Reads SPF, DKIM and DMARC results straight from the message's own authentication headers.

  5. 05

    Risk scoring

    Weighs every signal — spoofing and malicious attachments count extra — into one 0–100 score with plain-language reasons.

What it actually checks for

Every number below is a real signal set inside the analyzer, not a marketing figure — you can open the source and count them yourself.

0 phishing-language phrase signatures

“verify your account”, “wire transfer”, “final notice” and 24 more, scanned across subject and body.

0 flagged top-level domains

Including .zip, .top, .click and other TLDs disproportionately used for abuse.

0 suspicious attachment extensions

Executable and script types like .exe, .js, .hta and .iso are flagged on sight.

0 brand-impersonation keywords

Catches lookalike domains such as paypal-secure-login.com that reference a brand without being it.

0 known URL shorteners

bit.ly, tinyurl, t.co and others — shorteners hide the real destination, so every hit is surfaced.

0 authentication protocols read

SPF, DKIM and DMARC results are parsed straight from the message's own headers — nothing re-verified over SMTP.

Runs entirely on the server that hosts it — no email content is sent to a third-party API.
